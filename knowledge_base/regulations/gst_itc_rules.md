# GST Input Tax Credit Rules for AP - Internal Guidance Note

This is an internal summary for AP staff of the Central GST Act, 2017 rules that decide whether the company can claim input tax credit (ITC) on a supplier invoice. It is not legal advice.

## Conditions for claiming ITC (section 16(2))
- The company holds a valid tax invoice from a registered supplier.
- The invoice appears in the company's auto-drafted statement GSTR-2B, which means the supplier has reported it in its own GSTR-1.
- The goods or services have actually been received (GRN or service entry sheet).
- The supplier has actually paid the tax to the government and filed its GSTR-3B return.
- AP checks GSTR-2B before releasing the GST portion of large invoices.

## Suppliers with a cancelled or suspended GSTIN
- A supplier whose GST registration is cancelled cannot issue a valid tax invoice or charge GST for supplies after the cancellation date.
- ITC on such an invoice is not available to the company, so paying the GST portion is a direct cost.
- AP pays the taxable value only after the Finance Controller's review, and withholds the GST portion until the supplier shows a revocation order restoring the registration.
- GST "input tax credit at risk" for a vendor = the GST amount on that vendor's open invoices.

## Suppliers who do not file returns
- If a supplier has not filed GSTR-1 or GSTR-3B on time, the invoice may not appear in GSTR-2B and the credit can be blocked.
- Under rule 37A of the CGST Rules, credit already taken must be reversed if the supplier has not filed GSTR-3B for the period by 30 September of the following financial year.

## The 180-day payment rule
- If the company does not pay the supplier the invoice value plus tax within 180 days of the invoice date, the ITC taken must be reversed with interest. The credit can be claimed again after payment.
- This is another reason why invoices stuck on hold for a long time must be resolved quickly.

## Freight (goods transport agency) invoices
- A goods transport agency (GTA) either charges GST at 12% under forward charge with credit, or the company pays 5% under reverse charge. The option chosen must be stated on the GTA's invoice or in its annual declaration.
