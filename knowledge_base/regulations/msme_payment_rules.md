# MSME Payment Rules - Internal Guidance Note

This note summarises the rules the company follows when paying suppliers registered as micro, small or medium enterprises (MSMEs) on the Udyam portal. It is an internal training summary, not legal advice. For an actual dispute the Legal team must be consulted.

## Who counts as an MSME supplier
- A supplier with a valid Udyam Registration Number recorded in the vendor master.
- The vendor master must store the Udyam number and the classification (micro, small or medium) shown on the Udyam certificate.
- If a supplier says it is an MSME but no Udyam number is on file, AP asks for the certificate and treats the supplier as an MSME from the date the certificate is received.

## Payment deadline (MSMED Act, 2006, section 15)
- The buyer must pay on or before the date agreed in writing with the supplier.
- The agreed period can never be longer than 45 days from the day of acceptance (or deemed acceptance) of the goods or services.
- If there is no written agreement on the payment period, the deadline is 15 days from acceptance.
- In ProcureGuard the deadline is set at MSME_MAX_PAYMENT_DAYS (45) from the invoice date, which is the more cautious reading for this company because invoices are raised after acceptance.

## Interest on late payment (sections 16 and 23)
- If the buyer pays late, it owes compound interest with monthly rests at three times the bank rate notified by the Reserve Bank of India, from the agreed date.
- Example for illustration only: at a bank rate of 5.75%, the penal rate would be 17.25% a year, compounded monthly. Treasury must use the bank rate in force on the day.
- This interest is not an allowable deduction when computing taxable income.

## Tax deduction rule (Income-tax Act, 1961, section 43B(h))
- From assessment year 2024-25, an amount owed to a micro or small enterprise is allowed as an expense only in the year it is actually paid, unless it is paid within the section 15 time limit.
- The rule applies to micro and small enterprises, not to medium ones, and does not apply to suppliers registered only as traders.
- The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026. The Tax team confirms the matching provision of the new Act each year; until then AP keeps treating late MSME payments as a tax cost.

## Reporting
- Specified companies file MSME Form 1 with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs every half year, listing MSME dues outstanding for more than 45 days and the reasons for delay.
- Amounts owed to MSMEs and any interest are also disclosed in the annual financial statements.

## What this means for ProcureGuard
- An MSME invoice that is fully matched and due within 7 days should be paid now, even if it needs Finance Controller approval because of its size.
- An MSME deadline is never a reason to skip the three-way match, the duplicate check or the vendor risk check.
